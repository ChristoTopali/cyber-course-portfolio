Reflection Questions:

Answer the following questions regarding the captured data:
What different types of cables/media were used to connect devices? Copper Straight-Through cable, Serial DCE cable, and Wireless media.

Did the cables change the handling of the PDU in any way? No. Physical cables simply transmit bits at Layer 1 and do not inspect, alter, or modify the structure of the PDU.

Did the wireless Access Point do anything to the PDUs that it received? Yes. The Access Point converted the PDU between wireless frame formats (IEEE 802.11) and Ethernet frame formats (IEEE 802.3), re-encoding radio signals into electrical pulses.

Was PDU addressing changed by the access point? No. The Access Point operates as a Layer 2 bridge; it forwards frames without modifying the MAC or IP addresses inside the PDU.

What was the highest OSI layer that the Access Point used? Layer 2 (Data Link Layer), as it bridges frames based on MAC layer addressing and performs media conversion.

At what Layer of the OSI model do cables and access points operate?: Cables: Layer 1 (Physical Layer). Access Points: Layer 1 (Physical Layer) and Layer 2 (Data Link Layer).

When examining the PDU Details tab, which MAC address appeared first, the source or the destination? The Destination MAC address appears first in standard Ethernet II frame headers, followed by the Source MAC address.

Sometimes PDUs were marked with red Xs while others had green check marks. What is the significance of these markings?:

A green check mark indicates that the PDU was successfully accepted and processed by the device.

A red X indicates that the PDU was dropped (e.g., due to an ARP lookup fail, non-matching destination MAC address, or packet rejection by an interface).

Every time that the PDU was sent between the 10 network and the 172 network, there was a point where the MAC addresses suddenly changed. Where did that occur? At the Router. When crossing a Layer 3 boundary, the router strips the incoming Layer 2 frame header and encapsulates the IP packet into a new Layer 2 frame with new Source and Destination MAC addresses for the next hop.

Which device uses MAC addresses that start with 00D0:BA? The Cisco Router (specifically its interface, such as 00D0:BA8E:741A, as 00D0:BA is Cisco’s OUI prefix in Packet Tracer).

What devices did the other MAC addresses belong to? They belonged to the hosts (PCs/laptops) such as 172.16.31.3 (0060.7036.2849), 172.16.31.2 (000C:85CC:1DA7), and 10.10.10.2.

Did the sending and receiving IPv4 addresses change in any of the PDUs? No. The end-to-end Source and Destination IP addresses remain unchanged throughout the transmission path.

When you follow the reply to a ping, sometimes called a pong, what happens to the source and destination addresses? They swap/flip places. The original destination IP and MAC addresses become the new source addresses, and the original source addresses become the destination addresses.

Why do you think the interfaces of the router are part of two different IP networks? A router’s primary purpose is to interconnect different networks and route traffic between them. Each interface must belong to the specific network segment it connects to in order to serve as the default gateway for that network.

Which IP networks are connected by the router? The 172.16.31.0 network and the 10.10.10.0 network.
